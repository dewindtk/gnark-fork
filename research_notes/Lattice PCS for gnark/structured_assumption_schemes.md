# Lattice PCS and functional commitments from non-standard / structured assumptions, or with polylog verification (status Oct 2026)

Scope: schemes that depend on non-standard or structured lattice assumptions (k-R-ISIS / k-M-ISIS, knowledge k-M-ISIS, twin-k-R-ISIS, vanishing SIS (vSIS), BASIS, PRISIS, ℓ-succinct SIS), plus schemes targeting polylog verification. Each one is judged as a possible replacement for FRI in the gnark fork (univariate PLONK over bn254 Fr, 254-bit prime r, X^d+1 fully splitting mod r).

Source-access note: ePrint returned HTTP 403 / a bot-challenge page for automated PDF downloads (curl and WebFetch on `*.pdf`). Abstract pages (`eprint.iacr.org/YYYY/NNNN`) did load. Full-text details therefore come from: the Wee–Wu PDF on the author's site (utexas.edu); the local copy of Akita (`resources/2026-1983.pdf`, which has a detailed related-work section and an audit appendix covering RoKoKo and Grand Danois); the RoKoKo GitHub README; Albrecht's "SIS with hints zoo"; and the Lattice Assumption Zoo. The CRYPTO 2022 archive PDF of ACLMT22 also returned 403.

## Q1. Assumption status: which structured assumptions are broken, attacked, or sound?

### Takeaway
The **knowledge k-M-ISIS / knowledge k-R-ISIS** assumption of ACLMT22 is regarded as **broken ("morally")** since Wee–Wu (ASIACRYPT 2023). Any SNARK whose knowledge soundness rests on it (ACLMT22 SNARK, CLM23's plain-model instantiation, FLV23) has lost its security argument. **vSIS** is unbroken for the parameters used in succinct arguments. It has few reductions, though, and a 2026 cryptanalysis paper finds weak keys in an integer variant of the vSIS commitment. The falsifiable q-type assumptions (k-R-ISIS, BASIS, PRISIS, ℓ-succinct SIS) have no known breaks. Most have no reduction to standard SIS for useful parameters.

### Cited Findings
- **Knowledge k-R-ISIS / k-M-ISIS, attack.** Wee–Wu "describe a general methodology that heuristically breaks the extractability of our construction and provides evidence for the implausibility of the knowledge k-R-ISIS assumption of Albrecht et al. (CRYPTO 2022) that was used in several constructions of lattice-based succinct arguments". They add: "If we additionally assume hardness of the standard inhomogeneous SIS assumption, we obtain a direct attack on a variant of the extractable linear functional commitment of Albrecht et al." — [Wee–Wu, Lattice-Based FCs: Fast Verification and Cryptanalysis (author PDF)](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~dwu4/papers/LatticeFC-Fast.pdf)
- How the attack works: the CRS preimages z_i = A⁻¹(D t_i) are used to build a short basis T of the kernel lattice of [A | DG]. Babai rounding then produces a short (v, c) with Av = Dc without knowing any small integer combination. It is called a "candidate attack in the sense that we do not know how to rule out an extractor that outputs the same distribution … using small integer x_i's". Footnote: "After communicating the attack to the authors of [ACL+22], Albrecht implemented and confirmed the attack [Alb23]", where [Alb23] is titled "Knowledge K-M-ISIS is false, 2023". — [Wee–Wu PDF](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~dwu4/papers/LatticeFC-Fast.pdf)
- Scope caveat from Wee–Wu: "our oblivious sampling attacks only apply to the extractability of lattice-based functional commitments. All of the aforementioned schemes still plausibly satisfy the standard notion of binding". Also: "The results … provide strong evidence against the plausibility of the knowledge k-R-ISIS assumption, but they do not appear to directly break soundness of the SNARKs themselves" (this refers to FLV23 and CLM23). — [Wee–Wu PDF](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~dwu4/papers/LatticeFC-Fast.pdf)
- Albrecht's SIS-with-hints zoo tags Knowledge K-R-ISIS **BROKEN**: "invalidated, at least 'morally'" by Wee & Wu 2023a, with a linked SageMath implementation. The Lattice Assumption Zoo also lists "Knowledge k-M-ISIS" as **Broken**, cites Wee–Wu ASIACRYPT 2023 (ePrint 2024/028), and names the ACLMT22 SNARK as the construction relying on it. — [SIS with Hints Zoo](https://malb.io/sis-with-hints.html); [Lattice Assumption Zoo: knowledge k-M-ISIS](https://latticeassumptionzoo.org/knowledge-kmisis/)
- **k-R-ISIS (falsifiable, non-knowledge).** The zoo gives it no STANDARD tag. The known reductions (to R-SIS when m > k, or when the system is efficiently invertible) "are not interesting for applications". No break is listed. — [SIS with Hints Zoo](https://malb.io/sis-with-hints.html)
- **Twin-k-R-ISIS** (BCFL, chainable FCs): tagged EQUIVALENT. Albrecht–Fenzi–Lapiha–Nguyen show it is no easier than k-R-ISIS. — [SIS with Hints Zoo](https://malb.io/sis-with-hints.html)
- **BASIS_rand**: STANDARD, as hard as SIS. **BASIS_struct**: EQUIVALENT, implied by k-M-ISIS. **PRISIS** (Fenzi–Nguyen): no easier than M-SIS when m = 2. **h-PRISIS** (SLAP): STANDARD. **ℓ-succinct SIS** (Wee 2024): no tag; it is implied by BASIS_struct, and SIS implies 1-succinct SIS. — [SIS with Hints Zoo](https://malb.io/sis-with-hints.html)
- Wee–Wu describe ℓ-succinct SIS as "a falsifiable 'q-type' generalization of the SIS assumption", "the 'weakest' of recent non-standard lattice assumptions used in functional commitments". — [Wee–Wu PDF](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~dwu4/papers/LatticeFC-Fast.pdf)
- **vSIS** (CLM23): the task is to find short u* such that Σ u_i* f_i vanishes at random points a_i. Function classes include univariate monomials, Laurent monomials, and multilinear monomials. Hardness "is heuristically measured by SIS … assuming the structure of F(A) does not make SIS easier". "Reductions to vSIS are described as rare". The only one known is worst-case to worst-case from IdHSVP, for n = 1 point and low-degree univariate monomials. The entry (updated July 4, 2026) records no broken instances beyond pathological function classes. — [Lattice Assumption Zoo: vSIS](https://latticeassumptionzoo.org/vsis/)
- The CLM23 abstract page says soundness relies on "(knowledge)-k-R-ISIS" from ACLMT22. It also states that vSIS is no easier than k-R-ISIS. — [ePrint 2023/1405](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/1405); [search summary of CLM23 full version](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/1405.pdf)
- Jyrkinen–Lai (PKC 2025), "Vanishing Short Integer Solution, Revisited": in the univariate, single-point, constant-degree setting (a generalization of search NTRU), vSIS is at least as hard as finding short vectors in certain ideal lattices. The degree is limited to a constant. — [ePrint 2025/360](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/360)
- **2026 cryptanalysis of the vSIS commitment.** Das–Mukherjee, "On the Hardness of some Vandermonde Knapsack problems" (ePrint 2026/1554, received 2026-07-29, revised 2026-08-15). It gives new attacks on Partial-Vandermonde Knapsack in power-of-two cyclotomics, combined with the Das–Joux attack (EUROCRYPT'24), and "extend[s] the attack to the integer variant of the vSIS commitment problem, demonstrating that certain instances are weak for specific parameter regimes". The abstract does not name CLM23, RoKoKo, Grand Danois or SALSAA. — [ePrint 2026/1554](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1554)
- Akita (ePrint 2026/1983) describes the vSIS-based schemes as "leaning on stronger, more structured assumption variants (vanishing SIS) that have received far less cryptanalytic attention". It contrasts them with Module-SIS, which has "worst-case-to-average-case reductions … an extensive history of cryptanalysis, and underlying the security of NIST-standardized ML-DSA". — Akita, local copy `resources/2026-1983.pdf`, §1 and footnote 4 ([ePrint 2026/1983](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1983))

### Inferences
- Any scheme whose **knowledge soundness** rests on knowledge k-M-ISIS should be treated as broken or unsupported for our purposes. This covers the ACLMT22 SNARK, the plain-model CLM23 SNARK, and the FLV23 SNARK. A PLONK PCS needs extractability (knowledge soundness), so binding alone does not help.
- vSIS-based schemes are "unbroken but under-analysed". Das–Mukherjee 2026 is the first published sign of weak-key structure in the vSIS commitment family. Whether it touches the parameters used by RoKoKo or Grand Danois is not established.

### Gaps
- I could not read the Das–Mukherjee full text (ePrint PDF blocked), so the attacked parameter regimes, the weak-key fraction, and the relevance to RoKoKo / Grand Danois parameter sets are unknown.
- I could not find any response from the RoKoKo or Grand Danois authors to Das–Mukherjee.
- I found no cryptanalysis specifically of BASIS_struct, PRISIS or ℓ-succinct SIS beyond the zoo's "none listed".

## Q2. Per-scheme fact sheets

### Takeaway
Only two families are concretely efficient (tens to ~100 KB proofs, millisecond verification): **RoKoKo** (vSIS, Rust implementation) and **Grand Danois** (vSIS, estimates only). Both are **multilinear** PCSs over a ~50-bit (RoKoKo) or unspecified small q. Neither natively supports evaluations over a 254-bit field. The 2022–23 algebraic FC/SNARK line (ACLMT22, CLM23, BCFL23, Wee–Wu) is asymptotic, uses structured/trusted CRSs, and either relies on the broken knowledge assumption or gives only binding, not extractability. The schemes with polylog verification from **standard** assumptions (FMN, SLAP, CMNW24, BCS23) have proofs in the MB to hundreds-of-MB range.

### Cited Findings

#### (a) ACLMT22 — Albrecht, Cini, Lai, Malavolta, Thyagarajan, "Lattice-Based SNARKs: Publicly Verifiable, Preprocessing, and Recursively Composable", CRYPTO 2022 (ePrint 2022/941)
- Claims a SNARK that is "(i) tentatively post-quantum secure, (ii) publicly-verifiable, (iii) logarithmic-time verifier, and (iv) purely algebraic structure making it amenable to efficient recursive composition". The core is a lattice vector commitment opening to constant-degree multivariate polynomial maps, based on "a new family of lattice-based computational assumptions which naturally generalises the standard Short Integer Solution (SIS) assumption" (k-R-ISIS). — [ePrint 2022/941 (search summary)](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/941)
- Preprocessing with a structured CRS (it is listed among "lattice-based assumptions with a structured CRS"). For degree-d openings the CRS has size ℓ^{2d}·poly(λ, d, log ℓ), so ℓ⁴ for quadratic. — [Wee–Wu PDF](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~dwu4/papers/LatticeFC-Fast.pdf)
- SNARK knowledge soundness uses knowledge k-M-ISIS, which is **broken** (see Q1). Wee–Wu also give a direct attack on the extractability of an integer variant of the ACLMT linear FC, assuming inhomogeneous SIS is hard. — [Wee–Wu PDF](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~dwu4/papers/LatticeFC-Fast.pdf); [Lattice Assumption Zoo](https://latticeassumptionzoo.org/knowledge-kmisis/)
- **Status: superseded / knowledge assumption broken.**

#### (b) CLM23 — Cini, Lai, Malavolta, "Lattice-based Succinct Arguments from Vanishing Polynomials", CRYPTO 2023 (ePrint 2023/1405, full version a "major revision")
- Main ingredient: "a new commitment scheme based on vanishing polynomials". Results: a Bulletproofs-style folding protocol for linear relations with polylog verifier; a lattice VDF; and a preprocessing succinct argument for NP with a linear-time prover. — [ePrint 2023/1405](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/1405)
- Two instantiations: "in the plain model under the knowledge variant of the k-R-ISIS assumption, or in the random oracle model under the new, but falsifiable, vanishing SIS assumption". — [Wee–Wu PDF, concurrent-work paragraph](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~dwu4/papers/LatticeFC-Fast.pdf)
- vSIS is now the basis of the RoK / RoK-and-Roll / SALSAA / RoKoKo line (ASIACRYPT 2024, ASIACRYPT 2025). Those works exploit "the row-tensor structure of F(A)". — [Lattice Assumption Zoo: vSIS](https://latticeassumptionzoo.org/vsis/)
- **Status:** the plain-model variant relies on a broken knowledge assumption. The ROM/vSIS variant has no known break.

#### (c) Wee–Wu lattice functional commitments
- EUROCRYPT 2023 (ePrint 2022/1515, revised 2024-08-29), "Succinct vector, polynomial, and functional commitments from lattices". It gives a framework for non-interactive lattice VCs/FCs: a VC from standard SIS; the first succinct FC for bounded-depth Boolean circuits; "a polynomial commitment scheme over Z_q[x]"; and aggregatable VC/FC. FC security uses the new falsifiable BASIS family. Commitment and opening size are poly(λ, d, log ℓ). — [ePrint 2022/1515](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/1515)
- In WW23, verification is linear in |f| except for linear functions ("the verification time in [WW23] is linear in the size of the function f"). The CRS is ℓ². — [Wee–Wu ASIACRYPT 2023 PDF](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~dwu4/papers/LatticeFC-Fast.pdf)
- ASIACRYPT 2023 follow-up (ePrint 2024/028): two FCs with fast verification after preprocessing, from ℓ-succinct SIS. The first covers constant-degree polynomials, CRS ℓ^{d+1}·poly (ℓ³ for quadratic). The second is a dual FC for bounded-depth Boolean circuits with polylog openings and a CRS quadratic in ℓ. Both are standard-model with a structured CRS, and give **binding only**: the authors show heuristically that their own degree-d FC "is unlikely to satisfy extractability". — [Wee–Wu PDF](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~dwu4/papers/LatticeFC-Fast.pdf)
- **Status:** sound as binding commitments under q-type assumptions. Not usable as a SNARK PCS, which needs extractability.

#### (d) BCFL23 — Balbás, Catalano, Fiore, Lai, "Chainable Functional Commitments for Unbounded-Depth Circuits", TCC 2023 (ePrint 2022/1365)
- CFCs open a function of several independently committed inputs while keeping the output committed. CFCs for quadratic maps imply FCs for circuits of unbounded depth. Only the width must be fixed, and opening size depends on depth. Pairing and lattice instantiations exist. — [ePrint 2022/1365 (search summary)](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/1365)
- The lattice version uses twin-k-M-ISIS (EQUIVALENT to k-R-ISIS) and a structured CRS of size w⁵ with openings t·log²w. — [Wee–Wu PDF](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~dwu4/papers/LatticeFC-Fast.pdf); [SIS with Hints Zoo](https://malb.io/sis-with-hints.html)
- A 2026 follow-up (Balbás–Fiore–Lai, PKC 2026, ePrint 2026/379) reduces the **pairing-based** parameters from O(λw⁵) to O(λw³). It is not post-quantum. — [ePrint 2026/379 (search summary)](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/379)
- **Status:** theoretical, with an enormous structured CRS. Not a practical PCS.

#### (e) BCS23 — Bootle, Chiesa, Sotiraki, "Lattice-Based Succinct Arguments for NP with Polylogarithmic-Time Verification", CRYPTO 2023 (ePrint 2023/930)
- The first interactive argument for NP with succinct verification that uses the homomorphism of lattice commitments rather than Merkle trees. It is built on a delegation protocol over "leveled bilinear modules". Security rests on **Ring-SIS** (standard). Verification is polylog(N) for N gates. The abstract reports no concrete numbers. — [ePrint 2023/930](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/930)
- Akita groups it with schemes that get "polylogarithmic verification from standard lattice assumptions [23,3,31], but either give no concrete parameterization or carry proofs in the megabyte range". — Akita §1 ([ePrint 2026/1983](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1983))

#### (f) Standard / mildly structured polylog PCSs (for comparison)
- FMN (Fenzi–Moghaddas–Nguyen, JoC 2024): ring variant of BASIS. **SLAP** (Albrecht–Fenzi–Lapiha–Nguyen, EUROCRYPT 2024): multi-instance Power-Ring BASIS, with a further reduction to Module-SIS. **CMNW24** (Cini–Malavolta–Nguyen–Wee, CRYPTO 2024): "transparent-setup construction from standard Module-SIS with knowledge soundness against quantum adversaries". Reported proof sizes at degree 2³⁰: **8.3 MB, 767 MB, 5.17 MB**. — Akita §1.2 ([ePrint 2026/1983](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1983))
- CMNW24 abstract: transparent, no preprocessing, Module-SIS, Fiat–Shamir in the ROM for the non-interactive version, a quantum knowledge-soundness proof. At L = 2²⁰ it is "about 2X smaller than FRI" and ~70× smaller than SLAP. — [ePrint 2024/281](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/281)

#### (g) RoKoKo — Klooß, Lai, Nguyen, Osadnik, Tucci, ePrint 2026/575 (preprint)
- Linear-time prover, polylog communication and verification, proofs "roughly 200KB". It claims a Θ(log λ) proof-size improvement over RoK and Roll (ASIACRYPT 2025) and ~100× faster verification than Greyhound at similar prover time. The technique is "committed folding" (commit to cross-terms) plus sumcheck-driven structured recursion. It supports "(tensor-)structured relations, such as polynomial evaluation and sumcheck relations". — [ePrint 2026/575](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/575)
- Binding assumption: **vSIS**. Akita: "Its vSIS key is not a uniformly random Module-SIS matrix, but the paper and implementation estimate its cost heuristically through the scalar SIS expansion". — Akita Table 1 and App. F.2 ([ePrint 2026/1983](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1983))
- Implementation (Rust, Apache-2.0, `cargo +nightly`, AVX-512 for speed). Ring R_q = Z_q[X]/(X^N+1) with N = 128 by default (128–256), "almost split" into degree-2 factors. **q = 2⁵⁰ − 2687**. Challenge parameter τ = 22, operator-norm bound 9.8. Parameter sets cover degrees 2²² to 2³⁰. The polynomials are multilinear (MLE evaluation claims). The README calls snark mode "highly experimental". — [github.com/lattice-arguments/rokoko](https://github.com/lattice-arguments/rokoko)
- Akita measurements (single thread): RoKoKo verifies in **4.8–7.4 ms** with proofs **≈115 KB** (excluding commitment). Its native field is ~50-bit. Peak RAM is 35.6 GiB at the largest size. — Akita §13.2, Table 9 ([ePrint 2026/1983](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1983))
- Challenge set: RoKoKo is among the works using fully or near-fully splitting rings with "only heuristic guarantees on these fold challenges". — Akita §1.2 ([ePrint 2026/1983](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1983))
- **Security mismatch in released parameters (Akita App. F.2).** The implementation uses k^{ℓ−1} instead of the correct radix-recomposition operator norm. For (k, ℓ) = (6, 4) it uses 216 instead of ≈262,176. Correcting this raises the extracted bound at the p26 root from 825,510,427 to 1,001,986,232,870, and the root instance then has "only 77.32 bits of estimated cost" against a 128-bit target. "All six" affected commitments miss the target. Separately, an integer-lifting condition r′β_w′² < q/2 fails by a factor of 38.37, and "Four of the five projected sum-check rounds fail this condition after correction". Raising commitment ranks does not fix that; it "requires a larger modulus, a smaller r′ or response bound, or a norm proof over separately bounded limbs". Akita reported this as rokoko issue #94. — Akita App. F.2 ([ePrint 2026/1983](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1983)); [rokoko issue #94](https://github.com/lattice-arguments/rokoko/issues/94)
- Predecessors in the same line: RoK, Paper, SISsors (ASIACRYPT 2024, ePrint 2024/1972), RoK and Roll (ASIACRYPT 2025), SALSAA (ePrint 2025/2124). — Akita refs [58, 59, 61] ([ePrint 2026/1983](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1983)). A follow-up, "Succinct Shortness Check Under a Few Kilobytes" (ePrint 2026/2146), swaps RoKoKo's unstructured JL projections for a tensor-train random projection to cut verifier cost. — [ePrint 2026/2146 (search snippet only)](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2146.pdf)

#### (h) Grand Danois — Kallesøe, Khoshakhlagh (Aarhus / Partisia), ePrint 2026/1196 (received 2026-06-07, revised 2026-08-21; preprint)
- A post-quantum **multilinear** PCS over F_q with polylog verification and proof size. It builds on Hachi with three changes: (1) **vSIS** for structured public parameters plus a sumcheck that allows succinct verification; (2) multiplication by fixed ring elements as rotation matrices, which makes verifier row-folding linear in ring degree d; (3) a modified Hachi/Greyhound quadratic relation so that norm bounds use JL projections (structured projections adapted from RoK and Roll), merging the norm and evaluation proofs. Estimated proof size is **~80 KB for 2³² evaluations**. — [ePrint 2026/1196](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1196)
- Akita lists Grand Danois's binding assumption as vSIS, describes it as using "Module-SIS and vanishing SIS", gives the estimate as "80–90 KB for N = 2³²", and notes "No implementation". — Akita Table 1, §1.2 ([ePrint 2026/1983](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1983))
- **Soundness bug in v1, fixed.** The first public version sampled the row-compression challenge c′ *before* the commitment that binds the recursive witness, so "a prover allowed to choose the committed witness after seeing c′ can target this kernel". The current version removes that reduction and samples field challenges after binding. Quang Dao is credited with finding it. — Akita App. F.1 ([ePrint 2026/1983](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1983))
- An unofficial third-party Rust implementation lists Grand Danois among 14 lattice-ZK schemes. — [SuccinctPaul/lattice-algebra-rs PR #6](https://github.com/SuccinctPaul/lattice-algebra-rs/pull/6)

#### (i) Akita (ePrint 2026/1983), the Module-SIS reference point
- Akita frames a trilemma: Greyhound and Hachi have small proofs from standard M-SIS but square-root verification; RoKoKo and Grand Danois reach polylog verification "by leaning on stronger, more structured assumption variants (vanishing SIS)"; the standard-assumption polylog schemes have MB-scale proofs. Akita: M-SIS, Õ(N^{1/K}) verifier for any constant K, 61–65 KB proofs for N = 2²²–2³⁰, verify 8.1–16.2 ms. Knowledge soundness is in the **classical** ROM; QROM is discussed as open. — Akita §1, Thm 1.1, §13.2 ([ePrint 2026/1983](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1983))

### Inferences
- In practice, structured assumptions currently buy ~1.5–2× faster verification than Akita (RoKoKo 4.8–7.4 ms vs Akita 8.1–16.2 ms) at about 2× larger proofs (115 vs 61–65 KB). That is a weak trade given the assumption risk and RoKoKo's known parameter mismatch.

### Gaps
- ACLMT22 concrete parameters (ring degree, q, proof size in KB): the CRYPTO archive PDF returned 403 and the ePrint PDF was blocked.
- CLM23 concrete parameters, and whether the vSIS points are in R_q or Z_q: full text not readable (ePrint blocked; the EPFL slides fetch failed with "socket hang up").
- The RoKoKo paper's own soundness model (ROM vs QROM) and ZK property: paper PDF blocked; the README does not say.
- Grand Danois's q, ring, setup type (the vSIS matrix is presumably public-coin structured, i.e. transparent, but this is unverified), ZK, and soundness model.
- "Lova" (believed to be Fenzi, Knabenhans, Nguyen, Pham, a folding scheme from unstructured lattices, ASIACRYPT 2024): my search returned nothing, so I could not verify it. From memory it is a folding scheme, not a PCS, which would put it out of scope.
- No verified scheme matches the title "Lattice-based polynomial commitments with polylog verification" beyond FMN, SLAP, CMNW24 and Grand Danois.

## Q3. Fit for gnark: univariate evaluation over bn254 Fr (254-bit, fully splitting), ZK, PQ, setup

### Takeaway
**None of the structured-assumption schemes is a drop-in replacement for FRI over bn254 Fr.** The practical ones (RoKoKo, Grand Danois) are multilinear, evaluate over a small native field (~50-bit q in RoKoKo), use vSIS (under-analysed, with weak-key results in 2026), have no stated ZK, and are research preprints with known soundness or parameter errors. The older algebraic SNARK/FC line either rests on a broken knowledge assumption, needs a trusted structured CRS, or is binding-only. Setting q = r (254-bit, fully splitting) is not a supported configuration of any surveyed scheme.

### Cited Findings
- RoKoKo's native ring is deliberately "almost split" into degree-2 factors with q = 2⁵⁰ − 2687, and its parameter sets are fixed at build time. — [RoKoKo README](https://github.com/lattice-arguments/rokoko)
- Fully or near-fully splitting rings "often do not admit rigorous challenge sets", so works in that regime rely on heuristic fold-challenge guarantees. — Akita §1.2 ([ePrint 2026/1983](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1983))
- RoKoKo's integer-lifting step needs r′β² < q/2 and already fails at a 50-bit q with corrected norms. — Akita App. F.2 ([ePrint 2026/1983](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1983))
- Grand Danois commits to multilinear polynomials "over F_q", with q tied to the lattice ring. — [ePrint 2026/1196](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1196)
- Wee–Wu's EC23 framework includes "a polynomial commitment scheme over Z_q[x]" (univariate), but it is binding-only under BASIS with a structured CRS. — [ePrint 2022/1515](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/1515); [Wee–Wu ASIACRYPT 2023 PDF](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~dwu4/papers/LatticeFC-Fast.pdf)
- Structured CRS (trusted setup in effect): ACLMT22, BCFL23, WW23 and Wee–Wu ASIACRYPT 2023 all use "lattice-based assumptions with a structured CRS". — [Wee–Wu PDF](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~dwu4/papers/LatticeFC-Fast.pdf)

### Inferences (not sourced; my reasoning)
- **Univariate interface.** A univariate evaluation f(z) = ⟨coeffs, (1, z, z², …)⟩ factors as a tensor product ⊗ᵢ(1, z^{2^i}). So a multilinear PCS evaluated at the point (z, z², z⁴, …) gives the univariate evaluation directly. The polynomial type itself is therefore not the blocker. The blocker is the field: z, v and the coefficients must live in bn254 Fr.
- **Field mismatch: two options, both costly.**
  1. Set the lattice modulus q = r. SIS-hardness estimates then need larger rank and norms at a 254-bit q, and every NTT and ring operation runs on 254-bit coefficients. The fully splitting X^d+1 mod r removes the partial-splitting invertibility guarantee for short challenge differences (the Lyubashevsky–Seiler type of bound), which pushes challenge sets into the heuristic regime Akita warns about. No surveyed scheme publishes parameters for this.
  2. Keep a small native q and represent Fr elements as limbs. The evaluation relation then becomes non-native arithmetic, with large constraint and witness blow-up.
- **ZK.** None of the surveyed abstracts or READMEs claims zero knowledge for RoKoKo or Grand Danois. A gnark integration would have to add masking or hiding commitments itself.
- **Recommendation for the report.** Treat structured-assumption lattice PCSs as **not viable** for gnark's bn254 PLONK in Oct 2026. For lattices, Akita (M-SIS, transparent, ~61–65 KB, ms-level verify) is the most defensible reference point. It shares the same field-mismatch problem.

### Gaps
- No surveyed paper gives parameters, benchmarks or security analysis for q ≈ 2²⁵⁴ fully splitting rings. The cost of option (1) is unquantified.
- QROM security of RoKoKo and Grand Danois: not established in any source I could read. Akita itself is classical-ROM only.
